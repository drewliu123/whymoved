Neil Irwin

Federal Reserve chairman Kevin Warsh on Oct. 28. Photo: Mehmet Eser/Anadolu via Getty Images

A capital spending boom linked to the AI-related buildout is powering overall economic growth. Understanding its outlook and risks is the Fed's crucial task in trying to calibrate monetary policy right now.

The big picture: That's the takeaway from both the strong Q3 growth numbers released Thursday morning and Wednesday's press conference from Federal Reserve chairman Kevin Warsh, who is trying to oh-so-gently pump the brakes in hopes of prolonging the growth cycle.

With debt markets that have funded the data center and AI buildout wobbling, Warsh and the Fed face an extraordinarily delicate balance.
