Mizuho lowered the firm’s price target on Circle Internet (CRCL) to $43 from $45 and keeps an Underperform rating on the shares. The firm believes AI personal assistant engines pose a “marked risk” to Circle’s medium-term revenue. Mizuho’s simulation shows that agents like Muse are likely to recommend steering away from USDC, with a strong preference for high-yielding FDIC insured alternatives, the analyst tells investors in a research note. However, if users insist on staying in crypto, the AI agents seem to prefer other platforms versus Circle, contends the firm. It sees a $4B-$9B potential medium-term downside risk to the USDC market-cap and 10%-20% to reserve revenue.

Why Micron, SanDisk, and SK Hynix Stocks Are Rising Overnight After Friday’s Selloff — and What Wall Street Sees Next
Ben Yoffe
Nov 01, 2026, 10:19 PM
Analyze
A+
A-
Add as preferred source

Friday’s brutal selloff left semiconductor investors with reasons to worry, but Sunday night’s trading suggests some bargain hunters are already returning. Micron (NASDAQ:MU) is gaining 2.6% after losing 5.3% on Friday, Sandisk (NASDAQ:SNDK) is recovering 2.6% following a painful 13.5% collapse, while SK Hynix (NASDAQ:SKHY) is climbing 3.6% overnight after suffering an 8% selloff in the previous session.

The recovery extends beyond memory stocks, with Nasdaq futures rising about 0.8% as tech shares regain some lost ground. Concerns surrounding expensive AI infrastructure investments and disappointing earnings reactions have weighed on market sentiment, leaving investors reconsidering valuations throughout the semiconductor industry. Meanwhile, oil prices retreated on Sunday evening amid renewed diplomatic efforts involving Iran, potentially easing inflation concerns and helping improve investor sentiment.

The combination of lower energy costs and bargain hunting may be contributing to the rebound, particularly as strong demand for advanced memory chips and favorable pricing conditions continue supporting the sector’s longer-term prospects.

Micron’s Memory Roadmap Gives Investors Reasons to Stay Bullish

Recent share-price volatility hasn’t changed Micron’s determination to capture a larger portion of the AI hardware market. During an October 21 presentation, the chipmaker outlined plans to introduce successive generations of high-bandwidth memory annually through 2030. Its roadmap begins with HBM4 in 2026 and progresses toward HBM6 by decade’s end, positioning the company to benefit from expanding demand for sophisticated computing infrastructure.

Another priority involves strengthening domestic supply chains through investments intended to improve access to critical semiconductor materials. On October 20, the company announced a financial commitment to Indium Corporation, supporting expanded minerals processing capabilities in New York. This initiative forms part of a broader program worth up to $3 billion, aimed at securing essential resources for future production.

Recent industry commentary offers additional encouragement regarding the stock’s potential over the coming years. Rival SK Hynix warned that shortages could persist through 2030, suggesting suppliers may continue benefiting from higher selling prices.

Turning to Wall Street, Micron has a Strong Buy consensus rating based on 28 Buy recommendations, one Hold, and zero Sells. The average 12-month price target stands at $1,621.67, indicating about 83% upside from Friday’s closing price. (See MU stock forecast)

Sandisk’s Strong Earnings Couldn’t Prevent Friday’s Selloff

SanDisk’s latest financial results offer investors reasons to remain confident about its prospects, particularly given the continued expansion of data-center storage requirements. The flash-memory manufacturer reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $11.03 billion, representing 378% annual growth and surpassing analysts’ $10.72 billion estimate.

Adjusted earnings reached $49.51 per share, exceeding Wall Street’s forecast of $46.67, while data-center sales jumped 61% sequentially to $4.8 billion. The company’s outlook also offered encouragement, with second-quarter revenue projected between $12.2 billion and $12.6 billion, putting the midpoint above consensus expectations of $12.29 billion. Adjusted earnings guidance of $55 to $57 per share, presented on a pre-split basis, also exceeded analysts’ consensus estimate of $53.35.

Analyst commentary following the report offered additional perspective on SanDisk’s prospects, with Goldman Sachs analyst James Schneider maintaining a Buy rating despite lowering his price target from $2,200 to $2,000. Schneider highlighted stronger-than-expected revenue and earnings guidance, alongside improving traction among hyperscale customers, with data-center operations now accounting for nearly 45% of sales. The analyst also pointed to the company’s $5 billion in share repurchases during the fiscal first quarter and limited NAND supply additions as reasons to expect the stock to regain momentum.

The rest of Wall Street is also showing little hesitation about recommending the memory specialist. Of the 17 analysts covering the stock, 15 favor buying and only two remain on the sidelines, earning it a Strong Buy consensus rating. The average 12-month price target of $2,080.59 implies about 81% upside from Friday’s closing price. (See SNDK stock forecast)

SK Hynix’s Record Results Strengthen Its Investment Appeal

SK Hynix enters Monday’s session with another argument supporting its recovery, following quarterly results demonstrating continued strength throughout global memory markets. The South Korean manufacturer recently reported third-quarter revenue of 104.62 trillion won, representing a 328% increase from the corresponding period last year.

Operating profit also climbed to 83.33 trillion won, benefiting from stronger component prices alongside continued investment in AI infrastructure. Higher average selling prices for DRAM and NAND products helped improve profitability, reinforcing expectations that favorable industry conditions could persist.

Perhaps more encouraging for investors is the company’s outlook regarding supply availability over the coming years. SKHY anticipates shortages lasting through 2030, while long-term agreements already cover more than half of planned output for 2027 and 2028. Such commitments provide greater visibility into future customer requirements, potentially supporting margins despite significant costs associated with expanding semiconductor manufacturing capacity.

Wall Street appears particularly confident about SK Hynix, with all 10 covering analysts recommending Buy and none assigning Hold or Sell ratings. The resulting Strong Buy consensus comes with an average 12-month price target of $265.33, representing ~78% upside from Friday’s closing price. (See SKHY stock forecast)
